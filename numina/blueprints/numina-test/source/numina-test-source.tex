
\paragraph{Notation.}
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a simple undirected graph on \(n\) vertices.
Its Laplacian matrix is
\[
L(G)=D(G)-A(G),
\]
where \(A(G)\) is the adjacency matrix and \(D(G)\) is the diagonal degree matrix.
The eigenvalues of \(L(G)\) are ordered as
\[
0=\lambda_1(G)\le \lambda_2(G)\le \cdots \le \lambda_n(G).
\]
The quantity \(\lambda_2(G)\) is called the \emph{algebraic connectivity} of \(G\).

\paragraph{Complete bipartite graph.}
Let \(a,b\ge 1\).
The complete bipartite graph \(K_{a,b}\) is the graph whose vertex set can be partitioned as
\[
V = V_1 \sqcup V_2, \quad |V_1|=a,\; |V_2|=b,
\]
such that every vertex in \(V_1\) is connected to every vertex in \(V_2\), and there are no edges between vertices within the same part.

\begin{conjecture}[Algebraic-connectivity maximizer for \(m=2(n-2)\)]
\label{conj:k2nminus2_maximizer}
Let \(n\ge 4\), and let \(G\) be any simple undirected graph on \(n\) vertices with exactly
\[
|E(G)|=2(n-2)
\]
edges.
Then
\[
\lambda_2(G)\le 2.
\]
Moreover, equality is attained by the complete bipartite graph \(K_{2,n-2}\), and hence \(K_{2,n-2}\) is a maximizer of algebraic connectivity among all such graphs:
\[
\lambda_2(K_{2,n-2})=2,
\quad
\lambda_2(G)\le \lambda_2(K_{2,n-2}).
\]
\end{conjecture}
